Peeling Lens

Degree-one peeling runs live in your browser on a random k-uniform hypergraph with M vertices (cells) and d edges (keys). Move the controls to re-simulate. Red runs got stuck (peeling failed); blue runs peeled every edge.

Notes

  • Notation follows the accompanying working paper: Qn is the number of degree-one vertices after n peeled edges, t = n/M, xc, ac, βk, τk, σk are the closed-form constants. Every black curve comes from the analytic formulas; nothing is fitted to the simulation.
  • The drift correction uses a first-order uniform-partner transition. It is an approximation to the Doob compensator, not the exact finite-graph conditional drift, especially for Plain mappings. The 99% survival cutoff limits but does not remove survivor selection.
  • Finite-size failure rates can differ from the limiting value. A threshold-center correction of order M−1/6 in the window coordinate is known for the configuration setting; this page does not assert a finite-size error bound for Plain mappings.
  • Out of scope: k = 2, rates of convergence, the rare-failure regime far below the threshold.
Part II

Failure-conditioned measurement and sign imbalance

Before peeling, the sketch can be summarized by its centered count energy, standardized to X. For every fixed sign vector X has mean 0 and variance 1 over Plain mappings. Peeling failure depends only on the unsigned mapping, so the signs cannot change which runs fail. They do change how strongly X is correlated with the failure score R. In the limit this correlation is ηkρ², where ρ is the sign imbalance. Looking at X only after a failure therefore selects a shifted distribution.

Notes on Part II

  • Only the Plain model is simulated here, as in the main theorem. Signs are deterministic: the first ⌊d(1 + ρ)/2⌉ keys get +1. Keys are exchangeable, so this is equivalent to any fixed sign vector with that composition.
  • Measurement: T = Σv(Cv − kdρM/M)², d̂ = T/(k(1 − k/M)), X = (d̂ − d)/sM with sM² = 2d(d − 1)/(M − 1). It is taken from the initial sketch, before any peeling.
  • The score R is the backward-weighted functional of Part I, evaluated one step before t* or where peeling stops. Its agreement with actual failure improves with M, but it is not exact at finite size.
  • Predictions use the analytic constants with c = ηkρM². The shifted coordinate zsh is a finite-size diagnostic from the configuration setting; its transfer to Plain is not proved. The stat cards give the limit-coordinate value z₀ for comparison.
